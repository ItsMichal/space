In the fall of 2013 I was a sixth grader at STEM School and Academy in Highlands Ranch, Colorado, and my class entered the Student Spaceflight Experiments Program. Every team wrote a proposal for a small experiment that would ride to the International Space Station. Mine asked a simple question: can you brew beer in microgravity, and does it come out any different?

The idea was less silly than it sounds. Fermentation is one of the oldest ways people made water safe to drink, so a crew far from Earth might care whether yeast still does its job up there.

## Selected

On October 8, 2013 the National Center for Earth and Space Science Education announced Mission 4. Out of 744 proposals, mine was one of the experiments picked to fly. Everything sat sealed in a small tube, with the ingredients kept apart until an astronaut mixed them on orbit, and a matching tube stayed on the ground so the two could be compared.

That week the story got away from me. It ran on ABC World News and Fox News, Al Jazeera America had me on for a live interview, Jay Leno made a joke about it in his monologue, and Gazeta Wyborcza wrote it up in Poland, where my parents are from.

## Flight

The launch slipped through the winter. The experiments finally flew on Orbital Sciences' first Cygnus resupply mission, which left Wallops Island, Virginia on January 9, 2014. The samples came home in March. The programme published my account of watching the launch, [My Greatest Moment of My Life So Far](https://ssep.ncesse.org/2014/03/my-greatest-moment-of-my-life-so-far-michal-bodzianowski-ssep-mission-4-student-researcher-highlands-ranch-colorado/), and the title still holds up.

## The Congressional Record

On July 3, 2014 Representative Mike Coffman entered a tribute into the Congressional Record. It names the flight as SpaceX-3 in December 2013, the plan at the time it was written; the ride in the end was the Cygnus in January.
