halmic h marks, round 3: layered readings

The original mark layers three letters: one drawn positively, one as a hook, one as the white between the strokes (see the internal reading note in internal/). These keep the same geometry and apply the same trick to the handle. Options 2 and 3 are the previous round, for comparison. The tiny diagram beside each shows which strokes carry which letter.

The existing mark, same tiles and sizes.

2. h!

Letters: h, !

The mark's "!": a bar with a dot below it, same bar height (140.54), same gap (41.5), set after the h so the pair reads like the "M!" lockup.

Critique. Previous round. The clearest h and the best small-size survival, because the h is plain and the dot-and-bar do the family work. But the m and the negative-space trick are absent: it is the original's dot and bar bolted on.

3. Plus joint

Letters: h (plus joint)

The mark's bottom bar meeting the long stem: here the shoulder is a horizontal bar that crosses the ascender and sticks out the other side, making a plus where the two bars overlap.

Critique. Previous round. The plus joint is a real echo of overlapping bars, and it reads h at 32px, but it is heavy, closes up at 16px, and says nothing about m.

6. hm ridge

Letters: h, m

The arch starts left of the stem, so it crosses it (the original's overlapping-bar joint), then a second arch carries on to a third stem: the h's shoulder turns into an m.

Critique. Fails. The arch poking out of the stem plus a second arch reads as a lowercase m (or 'tm' at 32px); the ascender is too short against the width to win. The m dominates, so h is not the primary read.

7. peg alt

Letters: h, m in the voids

An h with one wide shoulder and a short peg hanging from its middle. The peg is the m's middle stem, so the two white slots either side of it are the m's counters, read against the stems the way the B is read in the original.

Critique. Works as a concept: the h reads (ascender, wide shoulder, leg) and the two white slots either side of the peg make an m you only see on a second look, the way the original's two white lobes read B. Costs: it is wide (253 by 235), the slots are 40 units so they fill in below about 24px, and at 16px it is an h with a blob.

8. M stem

Letters: h, M

The original M (stem, V, stem) at x-height with its left stem pulled up into an ascender: one glyph, read as an M or as an h.

Critique. Fails the brief. It is the original M with a taller left stem, and it reads M first and h second. Kept to show why: a V cannot be an arch.

9. hook left

Letters: h, J hook, !

The ascender curls into the original's J hook (the stem bottom runs into a bar going left, outside the letter), and the "!" dot sits bottom right at the original's 41.5 gap. The counter stays open, so it still reads h.

Critique. A clear h at 32px with a period. The J hook is a nice private joke on the stem's foot, but it is a flag more than a hook at small sizes, and the dot makes it 'h.' rather than 'h!'. No m in it.

10. M ridge recommended

Letters: h, M diagonals

The original's two diagonals turned over into a pointed shoulder: stem, ridge, leg. An h whose arch is the M's strokes.

Critique. The strongest h of the new set: tall stem, pointed shoulder, one leg, compact (166 by 235), and it holds at 16px. The shoulder is made of the original's two diagonals turned over, so the M is there for anyone who knows the old mark, but nothing in it is an m on its own.

11. hi

Letters: h, i

A plain h with the "!" dot floating top right at the original's distance: the dot gives a hidden i beside the h (h, i, as in hi).

Critique. Clear h at every size, and the floating dot rhymes with the original's dot. But it is only an h with a dot; the dot reads as an apostrophe or a stray mark, and there is no m.

12. h, m stacked

Letters: h, m

Like 6, but the middle stem stops short and a dot sits under it at the original's gap: the middle leg is a "!" hanging inside the m.

Critique. Reads 'hm' with two dots, busy at 32px, muddy at 16px. The idea (a hanging '!' inside the m) is too clever for the size.

13. ridge peg

Letters: h, M diagonals, m in the voids

The M's diagonals form the shoulder (as in 10), and a short peg hangs from the ridge: the two white triangles-and-slots either side of it are the m's counters against the stem, while the diagonals carry the M.

Critique. The peg inside a steep ridge is the closest this gets to the original's layering (M diagonals positive, m in the white slots), and at large size it is rewarding. At 32px the peg fuses with the ridge and the mark is a lumpy h with no visible slots. Too much going on in a 235-unit square.

14. double ridge alternative

Letters: h, M, m

Two ridges, three stems: the M's diagonals turned over twice, so the h's shoulder is also an m made of the original's strokes.

Critique. The best answer to 'incorporate the m more': two ridges over three stems, so it is h and m at once, built only from the original's diagonals and stems. Risk: the m wins at a glance and a stranger may read 'hm' or 'm'. The ascender saves it at 32px; at 16px it is a small m with a tall stem.
